```sh
curl -L --create-dirs -o ~/Chromatik/Packages/chromatik-mcp.jar \
  https://github.com/oveddan/chromatik-mcp/releases/latest/download/chromatik-mcp.jar
# enable Chromatik-MCP in Preferences → Plugins, restart, then:
claude mcp add --transport http chromatik "http://127.0.0.1:$(jq -r .port ~/.chromatik-mcp/status.json)/mcp"
```

Any MCP client — Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Cursor, your own orchestrator —
connects straight into the running engine. No project-file editing, no reload
cycle, no separate server: the agent reads and mutates the same live show your
console renders.

## Parse the scene

A project you inherited is a few thousand parameters deep, and "how is this
wired" is an answer spread across channels, modulators, and mappings. The
agent walks the whole tree and tells you.

<div class="chat-demo not-content">
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>I just opened this project. How is it put together?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Eight channels on the master bus; the top three are grouped as
    Structure and blend Screen over a static base. Two LFOs and a beat
    follower drive nine modulation mappings — mostly speed and brightness on
    that group. The palette runs a four-swatch cycle that six of the patterns
    read from, so they shift together. Two channels are muted, and one
    — Drop — is a trigger-only blend parked at zero.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>What does the Drop channel do — and how is it triggered?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>It's your payoff moment: a blend channel that sits dark until
    triggered — by a MIDI note from the song, or pad A16 on your controller.
    Either one fires an envelope that takes the channel to full in 250ms,
    holds two seconds, and eases out over three — retriggering five effect
    envelopes on the way up.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>What can I map from the lighting console over OSC?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">

## Compose

Describe a look and it builds the structure; ask for motion and it wires the
modulation. The difference between a static change and a musical one is a
sentence.

<div class="chat-demo not-content">
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Build a smooth wash over the whole structure — soft sparkles riding
    on top, and drift to a new color every minute.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Done: a gradient wash breathing on a slow LFO, a sparkle layer that's
    silent at rest and shimmers on the beat pulse, and the palette
    auto-cycling through four saved swatches — sixty seconds each,
    five-second blends. The wash follows the palette, so the whole look
    drifts together.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Give the edge trace a pulse on every kick, and dip the brightness
    when the clap hits.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Wired the kick follower to position at full depth, and the clap onto
    brightness with negative depth — full at rest, a 30% dip on each
    hit.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Softer. It should breathe, not blink.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Halved both depths and eased the envelope release — it sways now
    instead of snapping.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-caption">
    Structure, motion, and palette in one conversation.
  </div>
</div>

## Perform

Ask for control and it hands you a rig you can play — macro knobs with OSC
addresses attached, tempo locked to the room, one-shot triggers that clean up
after themselves.

<div class="chat-demo not-content">
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Map the first knob on my controller to a global intensity — and give
    every pattern its own take on what intensity means.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Added an Intensity macro and wired your knob to it. Then, per
    pattern: on the wash it drives brightness and sparkle density; on the
    tunnel, speed and trail length; on the grid, contrast with a touch of
    strobe. One knob, eight interpretations — pull it up and the whole show
    leans in.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Sync the tempo to the CDJs and put the beat in the strobes.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Clock source switched to OSC — locked to the deck. The strobes are
    tempo-synced now, quarter notes; say the word and I'll halve them at the
    build.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Fire the drop so I can see it.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Fired — watch the rig. It's a one-shot; it cleans up after itself.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-caption">
    The mapping is part of the composition.
  </div>
</div>

## Debug

The show is running, something is wrong, and the cause is four panels deep.
The agent reads the same state you do, without clicking through it.

<div class="chat-demo not-content">
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Nothing's showing. Why?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>Output is live and your fader's up — but the pattern's surface
    selector is set to Cylinder. You're staring at the cube. Switched it:
    there's your trace.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line human">
    <span class="chat-who">you</span>
    <p>Why is the second channel washing out the first?</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-line agent">
    <span class="chat-who">agent</span>
    <p>It's blending Add at a full fader, so nothing underneath survives.
    Switched it to Screen and pulled it to 70% — the wash reads through
    now.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="chat-caption">
    Debugging and discovery are the same conversation.
  </div>
</div>
